# Presence is relayed over the Document's WebSocket, and an Agent's working area is its last write's bounds

ADR-0086 moved two things into M1 (#232): people editing one Document see each other's cursor and Selection, labelled with the User (F-COLLAB-05), and each Agent shows the area it is working on and the `intent` of its last write (F-COLLAB-04). The M1 exit benchmark has two browser Users and two Agents from different MCP clients on one Document (REQUIREMENTS §9, #233). This ADR records what travels, who sends it, what the browser draws, and how each Actor gets its label. Illustrator has no live co-editing, so the drawing follows Figma's and Google Docs' conventions where Illustrator has none.

MCP is untouched (ADR-0006): presence never goes through `/mcp`, no tool reads it, and an Agent learns of other Actors' work through `doc_changes` as before. Selection stays browser state, not Document state (CONTEXT.md).

## Decision

1. **Presence is relayed, never stored.** A browser sends `{type: "presence", cursor, selection?}` on the socket it subscribed with (ADR-0009). The Document Durable Object forwards it at once to every other socket of the Document as `{type: "presence", peer, actor, cursor, selection?}`. It writes nothing, opens no Transaction, changes no `rev` and sends no answer. A presence message is not a Command (ADR-0010), so a viewer's socket may send one: a viewer can select (ADR-0047). The DO is the trust boundary, so it parses presence as strictly as a Command: `cursor` is `{x, y}` of finite numbers or null, `selection`, when given, at most 1 000 strings of at most 64 characters. Anything else closes the socket with 1007, as a bad Command does.
2. **A Peer is one connection.** The DO gives each socket a random `peer` id when it accepts it and keeps it in the socket's attachment beside the Actor, User and Role, so it survives hibernation; a socket attached without one takes the existing 1012 path and reconnects. The `document` message gains `peers: {peer, actor}[]`, every other open socket. After accepting a socket the DO sends every other socket `{type: "joined", peer, actor}`. Every way a socket leaves sends the other sockets `{type: "left", peer}` first: the browser closing it, an error, and each close the DO makes itself (1007, 1012, 4003 from `disconnect`), through one close helper in the DO. 4004 from `destroy` needs none, since every socket closes. A browser drops all Peers when its own socket reconnects and starts again from the new `document`. A browser answers each `joined` by sending its presence, changed or not, cursor and Selection both, within the next interval. So the DO needs no presence state that hibernation would lose, and a new tab sees every Peer's cursor and Selection within one round trip. Two tabs of one User are two Peers with the same label.
3. **Document coordinates and Node ids.** `cursor` is the pointer's position in document coordinates (pt), or null when the pointer is off the canvas, the window is hidden, or the tab shows another Document. Peers zoom and scroll independently, so screen coordinates would mean nothing to them. `selection` is the ids of the browser's Selection, its first 1 000 when it has more; an outline of more than that tells a Peer nothing a marquee would not. It is left out when the Selection has not changed since the last message, and the receiving browser keeps each Peer's last one, so a cursor move does not carry the Selection again. A browser sends at most one presence message every 50 ms (`PRESENCE_INTERVAL` in `sync`), and only when something changed or a `joined` asks; the last change in an interval is sent at its end, so a Peer always sees where the pointer stopped.
4. **An Agent's Working Area is the bounds of its last write.** The DO already computes every write's receipt `bounds` in one place, `receipt`, whatever the entry point (CONTEXT.md, WriteReceipt). The `tx` message gains that `bounds`. A write staged in an open Transaction, which ADR-0009 does not broadcast, now sends `{type: "staged", txId, actor, intent, bounds}`: the area and the sentence, never the staged Nodes. So people see where an Agent is working while a long Transaction is still open. The browser keeps, for each Agent Actor, the `bounds`, `intent` and arrival time of its last `tx` or `staged` message. A null `bounds` keeps the previous area and a null `intent` the previous sentence, so a `tx_commit` without its own `intent` does not blank the label as the work lands. A rollback or an expired Transaction sends nothing; the area goes at the 5-minute mark of decision 5. An Agent Actor is an Actor whose `kind` is `agent` in the labels of decision 6; an Actor with no row there is an Agent unless its id is `user` or `user_<userId>`, the two forms ADR-0047 gives User Actors, so DEV_TOKENS Agents count.
5. **What the browser draws.** Every Actor gets one colour from a fixed palette of 8, picked by a hash of its Actor id, so all browsers agree without coordinating, and its User cursor, Selection and Agent area share it. The drawings live in the overlay above the artwork that draws the Selection; nothing in them is hit, selected or exported.
   - A Peer's cursor is an arrow at `cursor` with a pill holding the label.
   - A Peer's Selection outlines each selected Node's bounds, 1 screen px in the colour, with no handles. Ids the browser does not have are skipped.
   - An Agent's area is its `bounds` with a dashed outline in the colour and a pill at the top-left corner: the label, then the `intent` on one line, cut with an ellipsis at 48 characters; hovering the pill shows the whole `intent`. It stays for 5 minutes after the Agent's last write, the idle time after which its Transaction would roll back, then goes. A tab that connects later shows an Agent from its next write on; the Delta Log keeps no bounds to show earlier ones.
6. **Labels come from the Actor rows.** `GET /api/docs/:docId/actors` takes read access (the `authorize` of ADR-0047) and answers `{actors: [{actorId, name, kind}]}`: the User Actors and Agent Actors, revoked included, of the owner and every Member, named as history names them (F-COLLAB-07): a User by login, an Agent as "Claude Code (woody)". A browser fetches it on connect, and again when a message names an Actor it has no label for, at most once per Actor per connection. An Actor with no row is labelled by its id, a User Actor without its `user_` prefix: dev mode writes no rows, so its browsers read `alice` and its Agents `agent-a`. History, when it gets a browser view, uses the same labels.
7. **Dev mode names its browsers by a cookie.** In dev mode a browser request with the cookie `kalamo_dev_user=<name>`, `name` matching `[a-z0-9-]{1,32}`, is the User `<name>` with the Actor `user_<name>`; without it, or with any other value, it is `local` with `user`, as now. No route sets it: the e2e suite sets it with Playwright's `addCookies` and `pnpm bench` (#233) as a `Cookie` header, so they can run two browser Users with two Actors, which history and presence tell apart. A Document such a User creates is owned by `<name>`, and dev mode's `authorize` already answers owner for everyone, so dev mode needs no Role changes. GitHub mode ignores the cookie.

## Considered Options

- **Presence through MCP: a tool an Agent calls to say where it works, or `notifications` to Agents.** ADR-0006 rules out sessions and subscriptions, and every tool costs each Agent tokens on every turn, which ADR-0086 is shrinking. The receipt `bounds` already says where an Agent worked, without the Agent doing anything.
- **A `workingArea` argument on write tools.** Agents would have to fill it, and an argument an Agent may skip is shown only when it bothers. The bounds of what it wrote are always right.
- **The DO keeps every Peer's presence and sends a snapshot on connect.** Memory goes when the DO hibernates, which is when presence goes quiet, and a socket attachment holds at most 2 KB, too little for a Selection. Answering `joined` costs one message per Peer and needs no state.
- **Only committed writes show an Agent's area.** An Agent's Transaction may stay open for minutes; people would see it only at `tx_commit`.
- **One presence per User rather than per connection.** Two tabs of one User would overwrite each other's cursor many times a second.
- **Labels in the DO, from Worker headers on every call.** Every Document RPC would gain an argument, and the names already live in D1, where history needs them too.
- **Live drag previews of other Peers, as Figma shows.** Each pointer move would carry Node geometry, and a Peer's preview would need the conflict rules a soft lock gives. Deferred with soft locks (F-COLLAB-03, M3); a Peer's drag shows when it commits.
- **A list of who is here in the menu bar, as Figma's avatars.** Neither F-COLLAB-04 nor F-COLLAB-05 asks for one, and the canvas already labels every cursor and area. It can follow if people lose track of work off screen.
- **A separate presence channel (Cloudflare Realtime, a second socket).** A second authentication story for the same people, and no ordering with the `tx` messages it refers to.

## Consequences

- `sync`'s `ClientMessage` becomes a union of `command` and `presence`; `ServerMessage` gains `presence`, `joined`, `left` and `staged`; `TxMessage` gains `bounds`; `DocumentMessage` gains `peers`.
- Cursor traffic keeps a Document's DO awake while anyone moves the pointer, so it does not hibernate then. A Peer sends at most 20 messages a second, the DO's parse caps each at 1 000 ids, and the 20-connection Quota (ADR-0048) bounds the fan-out at 19 copies of each. Cloudflare bills incoming WebSocket messages at 20 to a request.
- F-COLLAB-04 and F-COLLAB-05's cursors and Selections are done when the tickets from #232 land. Comments (F-COLLAB-05, P2) and soft locks (F-COLLAB-03, M3) are not part of this ADR.
- The browser parts are tested as pure functions of the messages (the presence state, which Agent areas show, colours and labels) and in e2e with two browser contexts that carry different `kalamo_dev_user` cookies. The relay and the `staged` message are tested in the Workers pool at the DO.
